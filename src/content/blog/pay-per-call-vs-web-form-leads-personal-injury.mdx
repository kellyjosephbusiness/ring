**Pay-per-call** means a law firm pays for phone calls from people who match its case criteria, instead of paying for web form leads. In personal injury, pay-per-call usually means one of two things: live transfers, which are screened before they reach your intake line, or pre-signed cases, which arrive with the retainer already signed. Firms choose calls over forms because the person is on the phone now, already screened, with no callback to chase.

## What is the difference between a web form lead and a call?

A web form lead is a set of contact details someone typed into a form. Your team has to call them back, reach them, and qualify them from scratch, often hours later.

A call is the person themselves, on the line. With a live transfer, they have also already answered your qualifying questions.

| | Web form lead | Live transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you get | Contact details | A screened caller, on the line |
| Screening | Your team does it after the callback | Done before the transfer, against your criteria |
| Speed to conversation | Whenever the callback connects | Immediate, during the hours you set |
| What you pay for | Every form submitted | Callers who passed your questions |

## Why do personal injury firms prefer calls?

- **The caller is ready to talk.** Someone who picks up the phone after an accident is looking for help now.
- **Screening happens first.** Callers who do not fit your criteria never reach your intake team.
- **No callback chase.** Your team spends its time signing cases, not dialing numbers that do not answer.
